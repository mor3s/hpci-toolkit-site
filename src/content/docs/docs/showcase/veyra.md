*Veyra is a tabletop role-playing game built on the toolkit, in which a real living plant sits at the table and the toolkit's grammar is played rather than operated.*

Veyra adapts the toolkit into fiction without discarding any of its structure. The three agents are all present, renamed but not altered: the plant is the *Witness*, a living plant physically at the table throughout a session; the machine is the *Loom*, a cluster of sensing filaments and light-bearing nodes grafted onto the Witness, joined for larger workings by the *Lens*, an immersive interface; and the humans are the players, whose characters carry these instruments through a failing world. What the toolkit calls a ritual, Veyra calls a *Rite*, and defines in almost the same terms the toolkit uses — not a spell and not a conversation, but a repeated, deliberate pattern of attention and response performed the same way each time because, over generations, it became the only reliable exchange anyone managed with these plants.

The six relationships survive intact as the moments a Rite moves through. The machine *says*, speaking a fixed line the Rite has always used; it *asks*, and here the answer is genuinely open and must be decided aloud; it *senses*, reading the real plant through the Loom; and it *acts*, answering in light — warm gold-green for an exchange working as it should, hard red for stress, pale blue-white for something dormant. The two relationships the toolkit cannot instrument, tending and attending, are dramatised precisely as the toolkit treats them. Some Rites ask the player to do something for the Witness with their own hands — water it, move it, offer it something carried from the road — and to say, aloud, that they did; others ask them to look closely and describe honestly what they see. Neither is ever skipped, and in the game's own words, if nobody answers, nothing happened and the Rite has nothing to say. The design commitment described in the abstract — that care and observation can only be invited and reported, never measured — becomes a rule of play.

Two features of the adaptation are worth drawing out because they illuminate the backbone itself. First, Veyra's *Signals* — Root-Thirst for how close the plant is to water, The Given Light for the attention it is receiving, Breath and Warmth for the surrounding air, The Coming Weather for barometric pressure, The Exhale for a chemical trace, and Contact for plain touch — are a semantic naming of the toolkit's sensors: each Signal is a category of what is being sensed rather than a particular component, which is the portable, hardware-independent vocabulary a shared ritual library reaches for. The game arrived at it first, out of narrative necessity. Second, the plant's agency is made load-bearing: the game uses no dice, because the moment a Rite reads the Witness supplies the uncertainty instead, and no one at the table — not even the game master — knows in advance what the plant will return. The reading is whatever the living plant genuinely does, and the story must answer to it. The toolkit's insistence that the plant is an agent rather than an instrument becomes, here, the engine of play.
