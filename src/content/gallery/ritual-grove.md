---
title: Ritual Grove
tagline: A wearable field system for participatory woodland regeneration, following a tree from seed to forest across seasons of volunteer fieldwork.
context: rewilding
order: 2
---

The Ritual Grove adapts the toolkit toward sustained ecological practice, and it names the ritual model explicitly as its foundation: a ritual here is not ornament added to scientific work but a repeatable, adaptable protocol coordinating human attention, machine sensing, ecological knowledge, and acts of care. The three agents are all present but scaled outward. The human is the volunteer or fieldworker, and the system assumes several at once, of varied experience. The machine is a wearable assembly — a forearm-mounted Field Bracer carrying an ESP32 sensor layer, a Raspberry Pi in a backpack running the ritual engine and an offline map, and a phone as the task-specific interface and camera. The plant is the agent most transformed: no longer a single organism on a table but a tree followed across its entire biography, from seed lot through nursery batch to planted sapling and monitored tree, and beyond any one tree, a whole regeneration site.

The six relationships reappear, redistributed across a longer timescale and a larger territory. Sensing runs continuously in the background — environmental and soil measurements sampled without interruption while people work. The machine acts and speaks through the phone's guidance and haptic cues, and asks through structured prompts that adapt to the task, offering a novice a definition and an explicit "unsure" option where an experienced user sees only a compact set of choices. Tending is the physical work the system is built around and deliberately tries not to interrupt: collecting seed, sowing, planting, repairing a deer guard, managing vegetation, each recorded as it is carried out. Attending is treated with particular care — the design has the fieldworker observe the plant or site before the machine reveals its interpretation, so that human judgement is exercised first rather than anchored to an automated reading. The toolkit's three-layer structuring of a ritual is stated directly: invisible steps the system performs on its own, meaningful human steps it pauses for, and conditional guidance that appears only when judgement, learning, or an anomaly calls for it.

The distinctive move of this adaptation is what happens to the toolkit's single shared record. In the base toolkit that record is the one source of truth about a plant and its rituals; in the Ritual Grove it becomes a provenance network — biological and ritual provenance together, tracing what happened to each seed and tree, where, by whom, under which protocol, and with what evidence — carried offline in a self-contained expedition package and later merged into a long-term spatial atlas. The tending and attending relationships acquire an ethical weight beyond the intimate encounter: the system is designed so that local experts own the ecological interpretation and the machine surfaces evidence and uncertainty rather than issuing verdicts, which is the same commitment the toolkit makes about care and observation — that they remain irreducibly human — extended from a single act to the governance of a whole practice. The same backbone that stages one quiet exchange in Veyra here coordinates the labour of bringing a woodland back.
